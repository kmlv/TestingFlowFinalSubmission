\section{essex}
\begin{itemize}
    \item One attendee (I forget which, maybe Jayant Ganguli) asked why reform proposals aren't already implemented if the problems are that severe. We should perhaps mention somewhere 2 reasons:
        \begin{enumerate}
            \item the incumbent advantage of a large ongoing exchange, and 
            \item entrenched interests in the present way of doing things, which brings rent to key participants.
        \end{enumerate}
    \item Andreas wonders whether Flow has a levelling effect on profits, relative to CDA where the sharpest traders may have a sustained advantage. I told him that we were going to have a look at the cdf for (signed) excess profit = actual profit - CE profit for each trader in each period.  (He suggested perhaps a Gini coeff.)
    \item Another attendee (maybe Christoph Siemroth) asked about shocks and how humans respond. \\
    I said that contracts arriving or expiring in mid-session are what we have in mind but haven't actually tried them yet. \\
    Yes, it was Christof.  He was thinking of traders acting on short-lived private info in common values setups, where speed is an important advantage.
    \item Relatedly, someone asked about fast traders. I mentioned the work in Cologne, and said we hoped eventually to connect to that. \\
    Andreas was looking for ways to simplify CSLOs that might improve Flow performance. He observed that the cumulative quantity Q may possibly be dropped when we are thinking about HFT settings: with common values and short lived info (public or private) about the next move or two. Probably they want to cancel and move on before any reasonable Q is reached.
    \item Friederike Mengel asked whether the incentive to acquire private info is undermined in flow markets, where it may be far less profitable than in CDAs. My reaction was, maybe this is true for shortlived info about the next price move direction, but the social value of that sort of info is tiny.  
\end{itemize}
